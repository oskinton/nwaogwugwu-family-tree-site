    Our story

      This family tree began as a typed booklet, Our Family Tree, put together after the Nigerian–Biafran war of 1967–1970 so that the family’s history would not be lost a second time. It was brought up to date for Homecoming 2026, and this page carries that update.

      The tree here starts with Ukomadu Nwaogwugu, his six wives and their twenty-three children, and follows each line down to the youngest generation on record.

      Why it was written down

      The opening page explains that the story was told in part by Mr. Isaiah Nwauharia, described as a historian by the family’s own standards, who received it from his father Nwauharia Nwaoko and his uncles Eze and Onwunali Nwaogwugwu. The war had shown how quickly records and elders could be lost, and the compilers wanted something permanent for the generations to come.

      Read the opening page as typed

      1. This narrative or collection is about our family tree whose Patriach is Agbighgba.

      It is told in part by Mr. Isaiah Nwauharia a member of the family, who is by our native standards a historian. This narrative has been passed to him from the following elders of the family Nwauharia Nwaoko, his father and Eze and Onwunali Nwaogwugwu his uncles.

      We can no longer leave the story of our family tree to fate, but must document permanently what is left now, as events like the recent BIAFRAN/NIGERIAN war 1966/70, demonstrated the loss of innumerable records through fright and flight for safety and the death of elders of the family which dealth an irreparable blow to accurate collection of our birth dates, that were passed by them to the sons and daughters of this family.

      2. We believe that we owe our sons and daughters the stories of their backgrounds and therefore must leave for our sons and the generations to come a permanent documentation of our family tree – the importance of which cannot be over stated.

      Our geatest difficulty in this maider collection however, has been in obtaining accurate date of birth, (DOB).

      What we have attempted to do is a humble beginning. It is hoped that this collection should be reviewed revised and reprinted to up date the family tree from time to time.

      How to read the tree

      • A filled circle with a number is a closed branch. The number is how many descendants are recorded beneath it. Select it to open.
      • A small filled circle is an open branch. Select it to close.
      • A hollow circle is someone with no children on record yet.
      • A small pencil beside a name means someone has added or changed something since the 2026 record. Select it to see who changed what, and when.
      • A dashed circle is a person who appears in two places in the record. Selecting it takes you to their main entry.

      Each of Ukomadu’s six wives starts a branch with its own colour, and that colour follows her descendants all the way down.

      The record marks some marriages and children as “through inheritance”. This refers to the custom by which a widow and her children were taken into the household of a kinsman of her late husband. The tree keeps the record’s wording wherever it is used.

      Names are spelled as they appear in the record. If a spelling looks wrong, or someone is missing, see Help fill the gaps.

      Help fill the gaps

      The people who typed the first booklet hoped it would be “reviewed revised and reprinted” from time to time. These are the places where today’s record is incomplete or unclear. If you know an answer, sign in under Contribute and add it, or pass it to whoever keeps the family tree.
